Tetris turns 40.

A universally recognised icon, played across every demographic, platform, and generation.

The brief was a full modernisation of the core mobile experience for the 40th anniversary.

Built to last another decade of live-ops, iteration, and global scale.

Interaction Over Interpolation

Stripping the visual language back to its core geometry forced an uncomfortable question: how much motion is too much?

Early explorations leaned toward heavier visual feedback, but A/B testing consistently showed that speed and clarity outperformed complex animation.

Sometimes less animation creates the best interaction.

One System, Six Studios

Coordinating a 4-year-old codebase across six global studios made misalignment a constant risk.

I led a centralized Unity motion library: reusable animation components any studio could implement independently.

Brand consistency at scale, without the bottleneck.

Built for What Comes Next

The framework I designed had live-ops and continuous iteration built in from the start.

Future teams can build on the core brand consistent motion language without breaking it.

This was a deeply collaborative effort across time zones and studios. At any hour of the day, someone, somewhere was working on Tetris.
